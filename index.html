Growth models

Two S-curves, in three dimensions.

Real growth starts slowly, speeds up, then levels off at a limit. Drag a model to rotate it. The depth axis shows how the curve changes as the rate r changes.

Drag to rotate · Ctrl+scroll or +/− to zoom

Logistic growth

N(t) =K1 + A·e−rt

Growth slows in proportion to N/K. The curve is symmetric around its fastest-growth point.

  • Carrying capacity K is the amber plane the curve approaches.
  • Fastest growth at N = K/2 (t ≈ 4.39 for K=100, r=0.5, N₀=10).
  • A = (K − N₀) / N₀
Drag to rotate · Ctrl+scroll or +/− to zoom

Gompertz growth

N(t) = K·e−A·e−rt

Growth slows logarithmically in K/N. The curve is asymmetric: it rises fast early and approaches K more gradually.

  • Fastest growth at N = K/e ≈ 36.8 (t ≈ 1.67 for the same inputs).
  • Reaches its peak rate earlier than the Logistic curve.
  • A = ln(K / N₀)
Amber dots are the sample observations

Solving the Logistic equation

  1. Start from the rate law
    dN/dt = rN(1 − N/K)
  2. Separate variables and integrate
    ∫ dN / [N(1 − N/K)] = ∫ r dt
  3. Partial fractions, then integrate
    ln( N / (K − N) ) = rt + C
  4. Use N(0) = N₀ to fix A
    N(t) = K / (1 + A·e−rt), A = (K − N₀)/N₀

Solving the Gompertz equation

  1. Start from the rate law
    dN/dt = rN·ln(K/N)
  2. Substitute u = ln(N/K), so the equation becomes linear
    du/dt = −r·u
  3. Solve the simple decay equation
    u = u₀·e−rt, N = K·eu
  4. Use N(0) = N₀ to fix A
    N(t) = K·e−A·e−rt, A = ln(K/N₀)

Use responsibly

What a forecast can tell us.

Data matters

Few or noisy observations make the fitted K, r and A uncertain, so forecasts are sensitive.

Models simplify reality

A single S-curve cannot capture sudden shocks such as a treatment change or a recession.

Forecasts are estimates

The uncertainty band assumes roughly normal residuals with constant spread. Treat it as an indicative range, not a clinical or financial conclusion.
